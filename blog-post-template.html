Writing & Life

On Writing, Becoming,
and Finding My Way Back

A few thoughts about writing, growing up, and the strange comfort of always having a story to return to.

September 11, 2026 5 min read

I have always found it difficult to explain why I write. There are practical answers, of course. I like stories. I like words. I like creating people who never existed and places I will probably never visit.

But none of those answers quite explain it.

Writing has always felt less like something I decided to do and more like somewhere I eventually learned to return to. Whenever life becomes too loud, I find myself opening a blank page and trying to make sense of the noise.

“No matter how life gets hard, I will always find my way back to writing.”

The stories we carry

I think every writer carries stories long before they know what to do with them.

Sometimes they come from people we have met. Sometimes from places we have lived in, books we have read, conversations we overheard, or moments that seemed insignificant at the time but somehow stayed with us.

And sometimes, they come from nowhere we can explain.

Learning to become a writer

For a long time, I thought being a writer meant having everything figured out. A finished manuscript. A perfect writing routine. A bookshelf full of published books.

I am slowly learning that it probably means something much simpler.

It means continuing to write even when the story is messy. It means allowing yourself to be a beginner. It means writing badly sometimes and writing beautifully sometimes and understanding that both are part of the same journey.

Perhaps becoming a writer is not a destination at all. Perhaps it is simply the decision to keep returning to the page.

So this little corner of the internet is my attempt to do exactly that.

To write things down. To share some of them. To keep others tucked away until they are ready. And, hopefully, to make another person somewhere feel a little less alone in the strange and wonderful process of creating something from nothing.
